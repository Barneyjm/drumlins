Starting points

What's real here and what isn't

The growth rates come from the linear stability analysis of Fowler & Chapwanya (2014), computed for their Figure 4 parameter set. Lengths scale with lD = √(η·u / (Δρ·g)), with Δρ = 83 kg/m³, the density difference between water and ice. With the link box ticked, faster ice enlarges lD but lowers Π. The theory therefore predicts that faster ice makes bigger, wider and less elongated drumlins.

In our tests New York and Puget showed the opposite: elongation rose with ice flow. Green Bay went the theory's way. The theory only describes how bumps begin. Their final shape, the blunt upstream end and the flat ground between them come from nonlinear processes. The isolation slider only imitates those.

The "measured" lines under each theory number come from outlining the hills on this map, the same way our detector outlines real drumlins.
